Independent causal marketing measurement for music

Measure what the spend caused — not what the platforms claim.

Indie labels and distributors pour money into ads that earn back a fraction in royalties, and justify it with “momentum” nobody can measure. Counterpoint is the independent, cross-platform layer that separates true incremental lift from the streams that would have happened anyway.

The team: a causal-inference economist (modelling & product) + a commercial lead with deep label relationships from Native Instruments (sales & partnerships). Two people, bootstrapped, built for profit — not a unicorn.

01

The problem

Five dashboards, five winners, one shrinking balance.

A streamed play earns a rights-holder roughly €0.004. A bought play costs €0.05–0.15. Labels spend into that gap every day on the belief that paid pushes trigger durable organic momentum — algorithmic lift, playlisting, real fans. They may be right. But no one can prove it, because every ad platform grades its own homework and claims the same streams.

In a typical campaign, the platforms’ attributed streams add up to more than the streams that actually happened.
Over-attribution is structural: Meta, TikTok, Spotify and the playlist plugger each take credit for the same play. The label is left allocating six-figure budgets on conflicting, self-reported ROAS and gut feel.

The question every A&R, marketing and performance lead is really asking has no honest answer today: did this spend cause anything, or would it have happened anyway?

02

Why now

The method is solved everywhere except here.

Causal marketing measurement — media-mix modelling, incrementality, geo-lift — is mature and even commoditised in other sectors. It is open-source for general marketers, well-funded and consolidated for podcasts, and crowded for brand-side influencer spend. In music, it simply doesn’t exist as an independent product.

Every music tool on the market is descriptive, not causal: Chartmetric and Soundcharts report what happened; un:hurd automates campaigns on heuristics; Spotify’s own Campaign Kit reports its own lift — the platform marking its own work. Nobody answers the causal question independently, across platforms.

A scale threshold keeps small players out — and a data lockdown keeps new entrants out. Both work in our favour.
Incrementality needs scale (the industry rule of thumb is ~€50k/mo of spend). And since Spotify locked down its API in early 2026 — gating deep data behind a 250k-MAU wall and banning use of its data to train models — a solo tool can’t get the data. A label or distributor already holds it. That makes the buyer the data source.

04

The customer

Sell to the aggregator, not the artist.

A single small release can’t support causal inference — too little spend, too little data. But a roster can. Labels, distributors and label-services companies pool dozens of releases, hold real budgets, and own the first-party streaming and ad data as it flows through their pipes. They are the customer and the data source. Pooling across the catalogue is what lets us beat the scale threshold no single client clears.

Considered & rejected

Series A B2B SaaS

Sits below the spend threshold where causal methods pay off, and the attribution category above it is already saturated. Wrong stage, wrong crowd.

Considered & rejected

Podcast sponsorship

Proves people pay for exactly this — but it’s already won by well-funded incumbents with data moats we don’t have. A cautionary tale, not an opening.

Music aggregators are the one segment with real spend, a genuine unmet need, and a structure that makes the hard method possible. That’s the wedge.

05

The product · MVP

One independent number the platforms can’t give them.

The MVP measures, for a release portfolio, the incremental streams, saves and net-new listeners each channel actually caused — net of the organic baseline — plus the marginal return on the next euro. All inputs are first-party to the customer: their distribution data, their ad accounts, their playlist exports, their release calendar. The deliverable is a per-release scorecard, a portfolio allocation readout, and one defensible lift number they can put in a deck.

01

Hierarchical MMM

Bayesian media-mix with partial pooling across releases — a six-week-old single borrows strength from the catalogue.

02

Geo-lift holdouts

Staggered launches across matched territories on geo-targetable channels, estimated with staggered DiD and synthetic control.

03

Calibration loop

Geo experiments become ground-truth priors that calibrate the model — how Meta and Google validate MMM. Transparent and independent.

Our defensible edge is not “MMM for music” — it’s rigorous causal inference under data scarcity: pooling, shrinkage, synthetic counterfactuals. (See the companion concept page for the full before/after worked example.)

06

Unique value

The one measurement they can’t get from the people selling them ads.

Counterpoint’s value proposition is a single sentence: the only measurement a label can’t buy from the platform taking their money — independent, cross-platform, and causal. It turns a budget allocated on five conflicting ROAS numbers into one defensible figure per release. Three things make it un-substitutable:

Independent

Not Spotify grading its own campaign. No incentive to inflate the lift it’s paid to deliver.

Cross-platform

One truth across Meta, TikTok, Spotify and playlisting — not five dashboards that double-count.

Causal under scarcity

Roster-level pooling estimates lift no single small release could support. The technical moat.

Why they’ll pay: the cost of being wrong is large and recurring. A mid label misallocating even 15% of a €300k digital budget is burning ~€45k a year on channels that don’t cause anything. Counterpoint costs a fraction of that and pays back inside a year — the willingness-to-pay isn’t aspirational, it’s arithmetic.

Concretely, every question below is a budget decision a label makes blind today. Each maps to a number we produce:

Is this release’s momentum real, or did we just buy it?
→ organic momentum index — growth net of paid-driven streams
Did the campaign cause streams, or would they have happened anyway?
→ incremental lift above the organic baseline, with a credible interval
Which channel moves the most value per euro?
→ cost per net-new listener by channel — not platform-reported ROAS
Does paid spend trigger durable organic lift, or vanish when it stops?
→ organic-to-paid multiplier and the half-life of the lift
Are the listeners we buy turning into fans?
→ cost per retained fan — 28-day repeat listeners by source
Will this release recoup its marketing and advance?
→ projected break-even streams at true incremental value + fan LTV
07

Beyond the wedge

One data asset, a ladder of products.

MMM is the entry point. The same first-party pipeline unlocks higher-value products in rising order of margin and moat:

  • Causal playlist valuation — does an editorial add cause durable listener gain or a three-day bump? The channel everyone calls hardest to attribute. Nobody does it rigorously.
  • Real-vs-bought momentum — an A&R signal separating organic traction from paid-inflated traction, to decide where the next marketing tranche goes.
  • Fan LTV by acquisition source — move the customer off cost-per-stream onto cost-per-retained-fan: saves, follows, repeats, and downstream live/merch value.
  • Tour & merch geo-routing — causal demand modelling to route tours and size markets. Live is where the money is, so this carries the highest willingness-to-pay.
  • Recoupment & advance-risk modelling — risk-adjusted return forecasting on a signing or release push. Structured causal forecasting for A&R capital. The premium tier, and the least crowded.

10

Go-to-market

Services first. Productise on repetition.

Don’t build SaaS yet. Land two or three friendly design-partner labels, fit the model on their last 12–24 months, run one geo-lift on an upcoming release, deliver the readout. This de-risks both problems at once: the customer hands us the data, and services revenue funds the year before any product exists. Productise the pipeline once the third engagement looks identical to the first two.

The single biggest accelerant is the network. Warm intros into labels collapse the slowest part of this market — the sales cycle. That is the commercial co-founder’s engine, and the plan is built around it.

11

What we’re honest about

The real risks, named.

  • Labels buy slowly and cheaply. The network is the mitigation — weight roles and equity accordingly.
  • The services→product transition is where measurement startups stall. Staying a profitable services-heavy hybrid at €1–2M is an acceptable landing, not a failure.
  • Spotify gives Marquee lift away for free. Constant downward pressure; our only durable answer is independent + cross-platform.
  • We are pre-validation. Every number here is a defensible hypothesis, not evidence. The plan’s first job is to test it.
